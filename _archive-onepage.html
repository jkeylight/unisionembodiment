02 — The Premise

We are not one thing.
We are many things at once.

The body feels one thing. The mind says another. The heart wants something else.

And underneath all of it, there is something watching — something that does not change, even when everything else does.

Most of us have learned to live in one dimension and ignore the others. We live in the mind, and the body becomes a stranger. We live in the body, and the mind becomes a noise we cannot turn off. We live in the heart, and the world becomes too much to bear.

Wholeness is not something to be achieved. It is something to be recognised.

The Five Dimensions

Body

The body is not a machine to be corrected. It is a field of lived experience — the place where sensation, emotion, memory and conditioning are held. It speaks in tightness, heaviness, numbness, heat and flutter.

Mind

The mind carries thoughts, beliefs, memories, interpretations and conditioning. Much of what we call "thinking" is actually remembering — replaying old learning in new situations. UNISON develops the capacity to observe these patterns rather than remain identified with them.

Emotion

Emotions are not problems to be solved. They are information — the body's way of saying: something matters here. Every emotion deserves recognition. Every action remains choice and responsibility.

Soul

The soul is the deeper dimension — the inward movement toward meaning, presence, surrender and relationship with existence itself. UNISON holds this dimension without imposing any belief. For some, the soul is experienced through devotion or prayer. For others, through nature, silence, service or love. All are honoured.

Awareness

Running through all of it is awareness itself — the capacity to observe without becoming identified. Thoughts are witnessed. Emotions are witnessed. The body is witnessed. This awareness is not a state to attain. It is the ground of being itself.

At the human level, we experience fragmentation. We carry patterns learned in childhood, burdens inherited from family lines, identities formed in response to pain. We live in the past through memory and in the future through worry, rarely in the present.

UNISON begins here — not by denying this human experience, but by meeting it with awareness.

At the deepest level, the self was never fragmented. What we call healing is often the work of bringing unconscious patterns into awareness so they no longer determine our future. This is real and necessary work. The body needs care. The nervous system needs safety. Relationships need honesty. Pain deserves acknowledgement. Dharma matters.

But at the deepest level, wholeness is not manufactured by the ego. It is recognised when the mistaken identification with limitation is understood.

03 — The Work

A session is not
a performance.

You do not have to be anywhere other than where you are. You do not have to be better than you are. You just have to come.

We begin with a conversation. I ask what brought you. You tell me what you can. There is no pressure to tell me everything. Some things take time to put into words. Some things never do.

Then we work with the body. Not to fix it. Not to change it. To listen to it. The body has its own language — tightness, heaviness, numbness, heat, flutter. We pay attention to what it is saying.

Sometimes there is movement. Sometimes there is sound. Sometimes there is silence. Sometimes there are tears. Sometimes there is nothing at all — and that is also welcome.

I do not interpret. I do not declare. I do not tell you what your body means. You are the authority on your own experience. I am here to hold the space, to listen, and to offer a steady presence.

The Six Movements

  1. Arrival We begin by settling. Not rushing in. The body needs to know it is safe before it will speak.
  2. Exploration What is present right now? Not the story of what happened years ago — the felt sense of what is here in this moment.
  3. Recognition We begin to see patterns. The same tightness. The same voice. The same way of holding the breath. Not to judge them. To recognise them.
  4. Embodied Exploration We stay with what is present. Sometimes with words. Sometimes with movement. Sometimes with sound. Sometimes with silence. The body leads.
  5. Integration Something shifts. Not always. Not dramatically. But something. We give it time to settle.
  6. Grounding We return to the present. A breath. A bow. A thank you. The session closes the way it began — with presence.

The process respects your pace. Your boundaries. Your capacity. Nothing is forced. Nothing is declared. The body leads.

This is complementary care. It does not replace medical diagnosis or treatment, and it does not claim to cure any condition. If something is acute or changing quickly, please see your doctor first. This work can sit alongside that care.
04 — Who Comes

People come for
many reasons.

Some come because they are tired of the same patterns — the same relationships, the same arguments, the same disappointments.

Some come because something happened. A loss. An illness. A transition. A moment that changed everything, and they have not been able to find their way back.

Some come because they feel disconnected — from their body, from their feelings, from other people. They have learned to live in their heads, and they want to come back to themselves.

Some come because they are curious. They have heard about this work, or they have tried other things, and they want to see what this is.

Some come because they are in pain. Emotional pain. Physical pain. The kind that does not go away, no matter what they try.

This work may be supportive for people who are

  • Exploring recurring family or relationship patterns
  • Interested in inner-child awareness and emotional healing
  • Navigating life transitions, identity shifts or hormonal changes
  • Experiencing emotional overwhelm, disconnection or a sense of being stuck
  • Interested in understanding their relationship with their body and emotions
  • Exploring inherited family conditioning and ancestral themes
  • Seeking greater self-awareness and personal integration
  • Interested in a holistic approach to body, mind and soul
  • Midwives and women navigating different stages of womanhood
  • Homemakers seeking reconnection with themselves
  • Anyone who feels they are not living their highest potential

Whoever you are, whatever brought you here — you are welcome.

Sessions are open to all adults. This work is complementary and is not a replacement for medical or mental-health treatment. UNISON does not diagnose, prescribe or treat medical conditions. Where appropriate, clients are encouraged to maintain or seek appropriate medical and psychological care. If you are in crisis, please seek appropriate care first.

06 — The Threshold

Nothing to perform.

The session closes with grounding and, for those who choose it, Naman — a bow of gratitude and surrender.
